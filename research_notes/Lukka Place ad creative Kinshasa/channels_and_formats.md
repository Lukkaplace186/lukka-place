## 1. Social media usage in the DRC: which platform leads, and which one carries commerce?

### Takeaway
Only a small, mostly male, urban minority of Congolese are on social media (DataReportal: 7.35 M social identities, 6.6% of the population, Jan 2025). Within that group, Facebook (with Messenger) has the largest ad audience, and TikTok is the fastest-growing (+53.9% ad reach in 2025). Instagram is marginal. In practice, people buy by finding an offer on Facebook or TikTok and then closing on WhatsApp, and Kinshasa property listings already follow that pattern.

### Cited Findings
**Audience size (DataReportal / Kepios)**
- DRC population 111 million in January 2025; 60.3 million cellular mobile connections at the start of 2025; 34.0 million internet users in January 2025 — [DataReportal, Digital 2025: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- 7.35 million active social media user identities in January 2025, equal to 6.6% of the total population — [DataReportal, Digital 2025: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- Start of 2024: 28.31 million internet users. DataReportal warns that figures are not directly comparable across editions — [DataReportal, Digital 2024: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- Digital 2026 edition: "30.0 percent of the DRC's total internet user base (regardless of age) used at least one social media platform in October 2025". The absolute identity count was cut off in the extract — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)

**Per-platform (DataReportal Digital 2026, data at end-2025)**
- Facebook: at end-2025, 37.1% of Facebook's adult ad audience in the DRC was female and 62.9% male. The total reach figure did not appear in the extract — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- TikTok: ads reached 19.3% of adults 18+ at end-2025, equal to 30.0% of the internet user base. Potential ad reach grew by 3.65 million (+53.9%) between end-2024 and late 2025 — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- Instagram: ad reach equal to 2.5% of the internet user base at end-2025 — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: DRC](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-democratic-republic-of-the-congo)
- YouTube: no DRC figure surfaced in search extracts (gap).

**Third-party estimate (not DataReportal; do not mix with the above)**
- NapoleonCat (September 2026): about 10.67 million Facebook users in DR Congo (9.6% of population), about 9.9 million Messenger users, about 1.08 million Instagram users — [NapoleonCat, Social media users in DR Congo](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/social-media-users-in-dr-congo/)
- DataReportal says ad reach is not the same as active users: it comes from Meta's planning tools and includes duplicate and inactive accounts — [DataReportal, Digital 2025 Global Overview](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-global-overview-report)

**Commerce: where selling actually happens**
- Radio Okapi (relayed by allAfrica, September 2026): online commerce is growing in the DRC. More and more sellers, including sellers of housing ("même logements"), offer goods directly to consumers on Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp and other platforms — [allAfrica / Radio Okapi, "Le commerce en ligne gagne du terrain"](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609200024.html)
- The same coverage stresses scam risk: fake offers, and deposits paid on Facebook pages for goods that never arrive. One buyer paid a $60 deposit for shoes — [Congo Quotidien, 19 Sept 2026](https://www.congoquotidien.com/2026/09/19/commerce-ligne-kinshasa-arnaques/)
- A Kinshasa e-commerce guide describes the typical purchase path as Facebook → WhatsApp → phone call → delivery → cash payment. Its "90% social commerce" figure is unsourced, and the guide comes from a vendor — [Systalink, Vente en ligne à Kinshasa 2026](https://systalink.com/vente-en-ligne-a-kinshasa/)
- A vendor blog argues WhatsApp is "the only marketing channel that works at scale" in Kinshasa. This is the vendor's own opinion — [Whakup](https://whakup.com/blog/rdc/whatsapp-canal-numero-un-pros-kinshasa)
- A June 2025 survey of 1,000 Kinois (Les Points, as reported in the press) found 69% get their news first from social networks — reported via search extract of [AfricaNews RDC coverage of Les Points médiamétrie](https://www.africanewsrdc.net/featured/mediametrie-les-gagnants-les-recettes-et-les-nouveaux-patrons-de-linfo-a-kinshasa/)

### Inferences
- In paid social, Facebook (feed plus Messenger/WhatsApp destinations) is the base buy. TikTok is the growth channel and reaches a younger, larger share of adults than Instagram, which is too small to justify its own creative.
- Facebook's DRC ad audience is about 63% male. Creative and targeting should not assume a female-heavy audience, and a separate women-targeted ad set may be needed to reach female decision-makers.
- Because buyers fear scams, a "verified agents" message matches the main buyer worry the press describes.
- The NapoleonCat (about 10.7 M Facebook) and DataReportal (7.35 M social identities) numbers differ by method and date. A planner should check reach in Meta Ads Manager before committing a budget.

### Gaps
- The absolute DRC Facebook and YouTube ad reach for 2026 could not be read: the DataReportal page was blocked in this session and search extracts truncated it.
- There are no Kinshasa-only platform figures. All are national.

## 2. How property platforms and agents in Kinshasa and comparable markets advertise

### Takeaway
Kinshasa property marketing today is informal: agency Facebook pages and Facebook Marketplace posts that show a USD price, the deposit terms ("Garantie 4 mois + 1") and a WhatsApp number. Comparable platforms elsewhere in Africa lean on performance digital (Facebook/Google), on-site banner sales and premium listings. I found no documented billboard or radio campaign by a classifieds platform. The one measured click-to-WhatsApp result for a property portal (Lamudi) favours CTWA over lead forms.

### Cited Findings
**Kinshasa (observed listing format)**
- Kinshasa agencies run Facebook business pages with phone numbers and addresses (e.g. "Idriss immo", "Kin Immo", "RDC immobilier") — [Idriss immo (Facebook)](https://www.facebook.com/idrisimmo.fc/), [Kin Immo (Facebook)](https://www.facebook.com/p/Kin-Immo-100054216441445/), [RDC immobilier (Facebook)](https://www.facebook.com/www.rdcimmobilier.info/)
- Facebook Marketplace rental posts in Kinshasa lead with price and entry costs, e.g. "Prix : 1000$ - Garantie : 4 Mois + 1", and give WhatsApp as the contact — [Facebook Marketplace listing, Kinshasa](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/item/1208551880735015/)
- Broker-style posts put the phone number in the title ("MIRACLE IMMO MULTI SERVICES 0852988835/WHATSAPP/APPEL") — [Facebook Marketplace listing, Kinshasa](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/item/958769496229961/)

**[Comparable: Philippines/Asia — Lamudi; the country of the test is not stated]**
- Lamudi A/B test, 16–30 September 2023: click-to-WhatsApp ads plus lead ads gave 53% lower cost per lead and an 8% higher click-to-form conversion rate than lead ads alone — [Instagram for Business, Lamudi success story](https://business.instagram.com/success/lamudi) (via search extract; the page could not be opened)
- **[Comparable: Kenya, 2014]** Lamudi's marketing was described as mostly "highly targeted (performance) digital advertising through platforms like Google and Facebook" — [Moses Kemibaro, 2014](https://moseskemibaro.com/2014/11/21/how-lamudi-became-kenyas-leading-online-real-estate-classifieds-website-in-a-year/) (old)

**[Comparable: Nigeria]**
- Nigeria Property Centre sells banner placements between listings on desktop and mobile for 1, 3 or 6 months — [Nigeria Property Centre, Banner ads](https://nigeriapropertycentre.com/banner-ads)
- PropertyPro says it has close to a million monthly visitors, sells branding to developers and construction firms, and offers a header strip banner — [PropertyPro, Advertise with us](https://propertypro.ng/advertize-with-us); [PropertyPro Placements](https://placements.propertypro.ng/)
- An Awka (Nigeria) study found practitioners use billboards, radio and TV alongside online channels, and concluded online marketing is more advantageous than those offline modes — [ResearchGate, Online Marketing of Real Estate in Awka](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358730433_Assessment_of_the_Influence_of_Online_Marketing_on_Real_Estate_Properties_in_Awka_Anambra_State_Nigeria)

**[Comparable: Ghana]**
- A Ghana study found Jiji and Facebook significantly drove completed property transactions, while Meqasa, Lamudi, Zillow and Instagram did not. It names lack of trust and the inability to verify transactions as key challenges — [AJHSD (Unilag), Digital Platform Adoption in Ghana's Real Estate Sector](https://ajhsd.unilag.edu.ng/article/view/3188)
- Jiji: basic listings are free, revenue comes from premium placement, and a verification system authenticates listings and identities — [Jiji Africa (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiji_Africa)

**[Comparable: Senegal]**
- Expat-Dakar (classifieds) earns mainly from premium listings and uses its traffic to build display advertising. A Senegal e-commerce ranking puts its traffic at about 355,100 against about 134,400 for jumia.sn — [AfrikaTech, Business and digital in Senegal](https://www.afrikatech.com/start-business/business-and-digital-in-senegal/); [ITmag, E-commerce au Sénégal](http://www.itmag.sn/contributions/e-commerce-au-senegal-les-60-sites/)

**[Comparable: pan-African e-commerce — Jumia]**
- Jumia's 2021 operating loss was tied to aggressive marketing, with customer targeting on Facebook and Google a major part of it — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/2021/08/10/jumia-q2-2021-report/)
- Jumia campaigns used storytelling and influencers, timed to moments such as Black Friday, to position Jumia as affordable — [Pulse Marketing, Jumia case study](https://pulsemarketing.africa/project/jumia/) (agency self-report)

### Inferences
- Kinshasa renters already read ads as "price in USD + garantie/avance + WhatsApp". A poster that leaves these out will look less useful than an agent's own post. Lukka Place can show them, but as separate costs, never summed (project rule).
- In Ghana and in Kinshasa scam reporting, trust and verification are the main buyer worries. "Agents vérifiés" is a differentiator that the informal Facebook pages cannot claim.
- Comparable platforms grow through performance digital, not mass media. Offline media is for brand trust, not for the bulk of acquisition.

### Gaps
- I found no source on Coinafrique, Jumia House or Meqasa ad creative and offers, nor any Meta Ads Library analysis of African property platforms.
- I found no Kinshasa-specific property portal case study.

## 3. WhatsApp status, click-to-WhatsApp, data costs, lightweight creative, 4:5 and 9:16

### Takeaway
Click-to-WhatsApp is the format that fits how Kinshasa buys. The only property-portal test found (Lamudi) cut cost per lead by 53%. Status is used as a free storefront and Meta now sells ads in the Updates tab, but I found no DRC rollout evidence. Mobile data is costly relative to income in Sub-Saharan Africa, so creative should be light: a still image or a short, compressed video. Use 4:5 in feed and 9:16 for Stories, Reels and Status, with text kept inside the safe zones.

### Cited Findings
- Lamudi CTWA test (see section 2): 53% lower CPL, +8% click-to-form — [Instagram for Business, Lamudi](https://business.instagram.com/success/lamudi)
- **[Comparable: Mexico]** Developer Lomas de Angelópolis reports a 33% shorter sales cycle with WhatsApp Business. The figures are self-reported — [WhatsApp Business success story](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/success-stories/lomas-de-angelopolis/)
- **[Comparable: Africa, vendor claim]** CTWA said to give 3–5x lower CPL than Facebook ads to landing pages. No supporting data — [Destinali](https://destinali.com/real-estate/use-whatsapp-to-generate-property-leads)
- **[Comparable: Nigeria, anecdotal]** Practitioners blame failed WhatsApp ads mostly on slow replies and drop-off at the price question — [LinkedIn post](https://ng.linkedin.com/in/growthmarketingagency)
- **[Comparable: Kenya]** A Kenyan guide calls CTWA Facebook/Instagram ads one of the highest-converting formats — [Webgaro](https://webgaro.com/blog/whatsapp-marketing-strategy/)
- **[Comparable: Kenya/East Africa]** Women entrepreneurs use WhatsApp Status as an informal storefront for stock, prices, completed work and promotions — [TechTrendsKE, May 2026](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/05/16/whatsapp-african-women-business-livelihoods/)
- **[Comparable: South Africa]** Meta introduced ads in WhatsApp's Updates tab (Status) and paid channel subscriptions — [Vutivi Business](https://vutivibusiness.co.za/business/new-whatsapp-features-to-help-businesses/)
- GSMA: median 1 GB price in low- and middle-income countries fell to $3.7 in 2024 (from $4.2 in 2020). In Sub-Saharan Africa, data often costs over 7% of monthly income, against the UN's 2% benchmark — [GSMA, State of Mobile Internet Connectivity 2025: Affordability](https://www.gsma.com/somic/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/The-State-of-Mobile-Internet-Connectivity-2025-Affordability-of-Internet-Enabled-Handsets-and-Data.pdf)
- GSMA: almost 1 billion people in Africa (63%) still do not use mobile internet. Mobile contributed $240 bn (7.8% of GDP) in 2025 — [GSMA, Mobile Economy Africa 2026](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-economy/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Mobile-Economy-Africa-2026.pdf)
- GSMA DRC report (Sept 2025): reforms could connect 9.7 million more Congolese and add CDF 9.8 trillion to GDP by 2029 — [GSMA press release](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/digital-reforms-could-connect-9-7-million-more-congolese-and-add-cdf-9-8-trillion-to-gdp-by-2029-in-drc-gsma-report-reveals/)
- DRC price per GB: one older Africa ranking gives about $0.88/GB for the DRC, among the cheapest in Africa. The survey year is not stated and the units are inconsistent in the article — [Techpoint Africa, mobile data cost](https://techpoint.africa/insight/mobile-data-cost-africa/). Treat as unverified. Cable.co.uk's 2026 table is the source to check — [Cable.co.uk worldwide data pricing](https://Cable.CO.UK/mobiles/worldwide-data-pricing/)
- Format specs: third-party guides give 4:5 as the recommended Facebook Feed image ratio (e.g. 1440×1800) and 9:16 for Stories and Reels (1080×1920). Meta's Ads Guide is the authority — [Sprout Social, Facebook ad sizes (Aug 2025)](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/facebook-ad-sizes/); [Jon Loomer, aspect ratios by placement](https://www.jonloomer.com/recommended-aspect-ratios-by-placement-for-meta-ad-creative/)
- Safe zones: one blog reports Meta unified Facebook and Instagram Stories and Reels into a single 9:16 safe zone in March 2026, keeping about 14% clear at the top and 20–35% at the bottom. This is not confirmed against Meta's documentation — [Billo, Meta ads safe zones 2026](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)

### Inferences
- **Recommended poster master sizes:** 1080×1350 (4:5) for Facebook and Instagram feed; 1080×1920 (9:16) for Stories, Reels, TikTok and WhatsApp Status. In 9:16, keep the logo, price and call to action between about 14% from the top and about 35% from the bottom.
- Lightweight creative: static JPG/PNG posters, or short video (under about 15 s) that still makes sense with the sound off, so a renter on a prepaid bundle sees it without a heavy download. This follows from GSMA's affordability findings, not from a DRC creative test.
- The CTWA call to action ("Écrivez-nous sur WhatsApp") matches the Facebook → WhatsApp purchase path. The Nigerian experience suggests answering fast and showing the price up front, so people do not drop off at the price question.
- A Status-sized version (9:16) can be shared by agents themselves at no cost, using the organic Status storefront habit.

### Gaps
- I found no DRC-specific CTWA or Status ad benchmark (CPL, CTR), and no confirmation that Status/Updates-tab ads are available to DRC advertisers.
- There is no verified 2025/2026 DRC price per GB.

## 4. Outdoor/billboard, radio and TV in Kinshasa; how offline drives online

### Takeaway
Kinshasa's outdoor market is being re-regulated (2025–2026). A freeze on new structures, a cadastral plan to remove at least 50% of non-compliant structures, and a push toward LED screens on main roads make billboards a higher-cost, uncertain buy right now. Radio is concentrated: Top Congo FM dominates. On TV, Télé 50 and RTNC lead the news. Offline works best as a trust-builder that sends people to WhatsApp or a short URL.

### Cited Findings
**Outdoor**
- In February 2025, Kinshasa banned new installations of advertising devices pending a survey and a new cadastre. The ban was still officially in force in February 2026 — [Decrypt-Eco, 25 Aug 2026](https://decrypt-eco.net/2026/08/25/rdc-kinshasa-impose-de-nouvelles-regles-au-marche-de-la-publicite-exterieure/)
- On 24 August 2026 the ARGPK (Autorité de régulation et de gestion de la publicité de Kinshasa) presented a cadastral plan: cut non-compliant structures by at least 50%, with six months for operators to comply. The plan phases out banners in favour of digital (LED) panels on main roads — [Decrypt-Eco](https://decrypt-eco.net/2026/08/25/rdc-kinshasa-impose-de-nouvelles-regles-au-marche-de-la-publicite-exterieure/); [Le Point.cd](https://lepoint.cd/publicite-a-kinshasa-50-des-structures-non-conformes-seront-retirees-apres-un-moratoire-de-six-mois/); [24 News Agency](https://24newsagency.com/kinshasa-un-nouveau-plan-cadastral-publicitaire-pour-mettre-fin-a-laffichage-anarchique/)
- A draft edict on outdoor advertising, including LED and LCD giant screens, was declared admissible in October 2026. It proposes two special advertising zones (the ring road, and the area beyond N'Djili airport) and a digital cadastre that geolocates every panel — [Congo Quotidien, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.congoquotidien.com/2026/10/21/kinshasa-edit-affichage-publicitaire/)
- Small operators worry about the cost of switching to LED — [Congo Quotidien, 6 Sept 2026](https://www.congoquotidien.com/2026/09/06/plan-cadastral-publicitaire-kinshasa/)
- The city tax schedule charges a higher monthly per-m² rate for panels on Boulevard du 30 Juin and other main roads (3/m² against 2/m² elsewhere; the currency was not visible in the extract) — [Leganet, Annexes AM 2020 publicité](https://www.leganet.cd/Legislation/Provinces/Kinshasa/taxes/Annexes%20AM%202020.%20publ.pdf)
- A 2020 IFASIC conference cited about 100,000 m² of advertising display space in Kinshasa — [Financial Afrik, Sept 2020](https://www.financialafrik.com/2020/09/13/rdc-8-millions-usd-de-recettes-de-publicites-pourraient-etre-tires-des-droits-et-taxes-en-2020/)
- When Orange RDC launched its signature in Kinshasa (May 2024), it used a motorised carnival on the main roads — [7sur7.cd](https://7sur7.cd/2024/05/30/rdc-apres-lubumbashi-orange-devoile-sa-nouvelle-signature-orange-est-la-kinshasa)

**Radio / TV / press (Kinshasa, Les Points surveys reported in the press)**
- Radio, first half of 2026: Top Congo FM 67% of respondents, RTNC 15%, Radio Okapi 10%, Réveil FM 5%, Digital Congo FM 3% — [AfricaNews RDC, "Radios: le règne sans partage de Top Congo FM"](https://www.africanewsrdc.net/actu/radios-le-regne-sans-partage-de-top-congo-fm/)
- Earlier Kantar TNS study (2017): Top Congo cumulative audience 22%, Radio Okapi 17%, RFI 17% — [Zoom Eco](https://zoom-eco.net/secteur-prive/rdc-top-congo-la-radio-la-plus-ecoutee-kantar-tns/)
- TV news, first half of 2026: Télé 50 78%, RTNC 75%, Numerica TV 61% — [AfricaNews RDC](https://www.africanewsrdc.net/actu/parole-ecrite-le-label-de-barick-buema-se-fraie-un-chemin/)
- Thematic channels: Zoom Eco TV led with 51% (ouragan.cd, July 2026) or 47% (zoom-eco.net, earlier). The two figures conflict — [Ouragan.cd](https://ouragan.cd/2026/07/zoom-eco-surclasse-les-medias-thematiques-avec-51-des-parts-daudiences-sondage-les-points); [Zoom Eco](https://zoom-eco.net/autres-actualites/rdc-zoom-eco-tv-capte-47-de-laudience-des-chaines-thematiques-a-kinshasa-les-points/)
- On X (May 2025), Radio Okapi had 1,440,035 followers, 7sur7.cd 1,113,192 and Top Congo FM 1,081,730 — [Numerico.cd](https://numerico.cd/2025/05/15/top-20-des-medias-les-plus-suivis-sur-x-en-rdc-radio-okapi-7sur7-et-top-congo-tronent/)
- Caveat: Les Points results come from press articles, several of them by the outlets being ranked, not from the original report.

### Inferences
- Billboards: avoid long new static contracts until the ARGPK cadastre settles. If buying, prefer LED rotations on compliant main-road sites (Boulevard du 30 Juin, the ring road), which the regulator is steering the market toward. Offline creative must be readable in about 3 seconds: brand, one promise, a short WhatsApp number or URL.
- Radio: Top Congo FM alone covers most Kinshasa listeners in the survey, so one station can test whether radio works. Spoken copy must say the WhatsApp number or a memorable URL aloud.
- Offline → online: put a WhatsApp number, a short URL or a QR code on every offline piece, and track it with a distinct number or URL per channel (the project already records taps and click-to-WhatsApp sources).

### Gaps
- There is no published Kinshasa billboard rate card (USD per face per month) and no LED screen loop pricing.
- No radio spot prices.
- No measured study of offline → online uplift in the DRC.

## 5. Language and tone: French, Lingala or a mix

### Takeaway
French is the language of commerce and media, and Lingala is the street lingua franca. Big brands localise their key line into Lingala: Orange RDC's "Orange est là" became "Orange aza". A French headline with a short Lingala hook is a pattern a major brand already uses in Kinshasa. For a marketplace whose buyers fear scams, proof (verified agents, real photos, a clear price) should carry the tone, with warmth or humour as seasoning.

### Cited Findings
- In Kinshasa, French is the language of government, education, media, public services and upscale commerce. Lingala is the lingua franca of the street — [Wikipedia, Kinshasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinshasa)
- Orange RDC's 2024 signature "Orange est là" was translated into the four national languages, Lingala "Orange aza", and accompanies all its campaigns — [Beto.cd](https://beto.cd/rdc-orange-rdc-lance-sa-nouvelle-signature-orange-est-la-a-kinshasa-apres-lubumbashi/); [7sur7.cd](https://7sur7.cd/2024/05/30/rdc-apres-lubumbashi-orange-devoile-sa-nouvelle-signature-orange-est-la-kinshasa)
- An academic book on Congolese advertising in Lingala says its specificity lies in the "génie de la langue" and in socio-cultural elements drawn from Kinshasa — [Abelela-E, La publicité congolaise en lingala](https://www.amazon.com/publicit%C3%A9-congolaise-lingala-Omn-Univ-Europ-French/dp/3841660495)
- In Lubumbashi, advertising (e.g. Vodacom M-Pesa) runs in French and Swahili, which shows the localisation follows the regional lingua franca — [Mémoire Online, traduction publicitaire à Lubumbashi](https://www.memoireonline.com/01/19/10549/m_Problematique-de-la-traduction-publicitaire--Lubumbashi16.html)
- Scam fear in Kinshasa online commerce (fake offers, lost deposits) — [Congo Quotidien](https://www.congoquotidien.com/2026/09/19/commerce-ligne-kinshasa-arnaques/); [allAfrica / Radio Okapi](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609200024.html)
- **[Comparable: Ghana]** Lack of trust and inability to verify are key barriers to digital property platforms — [AJHSD](https://ajhsd.unilag.edu.ng/article/view/3188)

### Inferences
- The headline and the factual line (price, commune, WhatsApp) should be in French, the shared written language of commerce. A short Lingala hook or tagline can signal "made for Kinois" without hurting comprehension, as Orange's "aza" shows. Lingala copy should be written or checked by a native Kinois speaker, not machine-translated.
- Tone order: proof first (verified agents, real listings, the price shown), aspiration second (finding your home in your quartier), humour third (e.g. the shared pain of the commissionnaire or "garantie" chase). Humour about scams must not imply that named people or groups are fraudsters.
- Project rule: user-facing French says "référence", never "repère". Place is written "quartier, commune".

### Gaps
- I found no A/B or recall data comparing French and Lingala ads in Kinshasa, and no study of humour versus proof in Congolese ads. Vodacom's Kinshasa Lingala slogans did not surface.
