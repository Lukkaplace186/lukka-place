# Visual style, poster structure and trust signals for Lukka Place ad posters (Kinshasa, 4:5 and 9:16)

Method note: research done 2026-11-25 with web search. Fetching full pages failed for every host tried (acp.cd, umvie.com, thejournal.ie, asa.org.uk, yougov.com, facebook.com: DNS blocked in this cloud session), so every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the full page. ## 1. Which creative patterns perform for property/classifieds ads, and what does Meta/Google guidance say?

### Takeaway
There is no controlled, published test comparing hero-home vs lifestyle vs price-led creative for property ads; the evidence is agency anecdote. What is well supported: mobile-first framing (4:5 feed, 9:16 stories), very little text on the image, one specific CTA, brand visible, and keeping key content out of the 9:16 overlay zones. Generic stock lifestyle imagery is reported to underperform.

### Cited Findings
- A search for controlled tests of lifestyle vs property photos in real estate Facebook ads found none; results were agency blogs and guides with anecdotal or vendor-sourced claims. — [Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/facebook-ads-for-real-estate/); [Krista Mashore, A/B testing real estate ads](https://blog.kristamashore.com/articles/ab-testing-real-estate-ads); [Walled Garden, split testing](https://walledgardenhq.com/blog/facebook-ads-split-testing)
- One guide says lifestyle shots of families in homes "often outperform" empty property photos; another reports ads leading with neighbourhood/lifestyle messaging beat ads leading with specs (anecdotal). — [Adlude, real estate Facebook ads 2026](https://adlude.com/blog/real-estate-facebook-ads); [AgentPulse, real estate ad examples](https://agentpulse.ai/blog/real-estate-facebook-ad-examples)
- An agency blog lists "generic lifestyle shots from stock photos" among underperformers at 0.6% CTR (method not disclosed). — [Blaccdot, best creative types for real estate lead-gen on Meta](https://blaccdot.com/blogs/best-real-estate-meta-ads-creative-lead-generation/)
- Testing advice is consistent across these sources: change one variable at a time (e.g. bright interior vs exterior view; agent photo vs property photo) and judge on cost per lead. — [Walled Garden](https://walledgardenhq.com/blog/facebook-ads-split-testing); [Krista Mashore](https://blog.kristamashore.com/articles/ab-testing-real-estate-ads)
- Third-party summaries of Meta guidance: keep on-image text short (one guide: about 6–8 words covering the offer and CTA), high contrast, text kept off the main subject; heavy text overlays reduce mobile readability. One guide cites "under 20% text overlay" but this could not be confirmed against Meta (Meta retired its formal 20% text rule earlier; treat it as a style heuristic, not a rule). — [Straight North](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-best-practices-that-convert/); [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-ad-creative-best-practices); [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/meta-creative-best-practices/)
- Pick one specific CTA per ad, matched to the funnel stage. — [HubSpot, powerful ads according to Meta](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/create-powerful-ads-according-to-meta); [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/fundamental-principles-creating-meta-ads/)
- Secondary sources attribute to a November 2022 Meta study a 1.2x–7.4x short-term sales lift from following creative best practices, and to Meta a 17% higher conversion-lift rate for campaigns mixing static and video vs static only (not verified against Meta's own pages). — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/how-to-improve-facebook-ad-performance-with-optimized-creative/); [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-ad-creative-best-practices)
- Meta suggests 1:1 or 4:5 for feed to cover more of the screen; 9:16 fills the screen in Stories/Reels. — [Madgicx, ad creative sizes](https://madgicx.com/answers/ad-creative-sizes-for-meta); [Adobe, Meta template guidelines](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/genstudio-for-performance-marketing/user-guide/templates/channel-best-practice/meta-template)
- 9:16 safe zone: commonly given as top ~14% (≈270 px of 1920) and bottom ~20% (≈380 px), sides ~6%; one source says bottom 20–35%, another recommends keeping content in the middle 65–70%. Two sources say Meta unified Stories/Reels safe zones in March 2026 (unconfirmed on Meta's site). Feed 4:5 has no UI overlay. Designing 9:16 with content centred lets it survive a 4:5 crop. — [Billo, 2026 safe zones](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [Madgicx, safe zone](https://madgicx.com/answers/meta-ad-creative-safe-zone); [AdNabu](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/); [Jon Loomer, aspect ratios by placement](https://www.jonloomer.com/recommended-aspect-ratios-by-placement-for-meta-ad-creative/)
- Meta can automatically add 3D/depth motion effects to static images in some Instagram placements (an Advantage+ creative enhancement) — relevant if the poster must render exactly as designed. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-ads-creative-optimization/amp/)
- Google's ABCD (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) was validated by Kantar scoring ~11,000 ads with Link AI; Google reports ~30% higher short-term sales likelihood and 17% higher long-term brand contribution for ABCD-aligned ads (model-based, video-focused, not a controlled trial). — [Kantar, validating Google's ABCD](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence); [Google, creative measurement gap](https://business.google.com/us/think/measurement/creative-measurement-gap-ai-google-kantar)

### Inferences
- For 5 posters, test structurally different concepts rather than five variants of one: (a) single real-home hero + one-line benefit; (b) problem/solution aimed at the Kinshasa "commissionnaire" pain (see §2); (c) trust/proof poster (verified agents, direct WhatsApp); (d) people/lifestyle with Congolese family at their gate or door; (e) neighbourhood-led ("Gombe, Limete, Ngaliema…" place names). Price-led only if a real, current listing price can be shown (house rule: never invent data).
- The "editorial / AI campaign" aesthetic (white field, huge sans headline, one photo, one CTA) is consistent with the low-text, single-CTA, high-contrast guidance; it also reads well on small screens. Brand recognition should come from the royal-blue block + wordmark, applied consistently on every poster (ABCD "Branding").
- For 9:16 WhatsApp status there is no Meta UI overlay, but if the same file is used in Instagram/Facebook Stories, keep headline/CTA/logo inside the central zone (avoid top ~14% and bottom ~20%).
- Do not rely on the 17%, 30% or 1.2–7.4x figures as Lukka Place forecasts; they are vendor-reported and video-weighted.

### Gaps
- No published, controlled real-estate test results (hero vs lifestyle vs before/after vs comparison split). No African/Francophone property ad test data found.
- Could not read Meta's own creative best-practice page (facebook.com blocked); no first-party Meta evidence on faces in static ads was found.

## 2. What trust signals matter, especially where scams are common?

### Takeaway
In Kinshasa the dominant fear is the informal broker ("commissionnaire") who takes a visit fee or deposit and disappears, or shows a different house; a fake owner signing a false contract is the other. Trust signals that answer those specific fears (verified agent identity, real photos of the actual home, direct contact with the agent, no money before a visit) are the ones to put on posters. Verified badges are the standard regional proptech answer, but no independent evaluation of their effect was found.

### Cited Findings
- Kinshasa residents told the state news agency ACP that brokers (commissionnaires) demand a visit fee and take people around even with no suitable listing; some produce a fake handover document before vanishing; one man was asked $10 "travel costs" to see a house and the broker disappeared after payment. Headline: the population has a poor image of property brokers. — [ACP, "Kinshasa : la population a une mauvaise image des courtiers immobiliers"](https://acp.cd/societe/kinshasa-la-population-a-une-mauvaise-image-des-courtiers-immobiliers/)
- A Kinshasa law firm raised an alarm (Oct 2026) over multiple fraud cases, including people posing as land/property owners who sign false contracts and disappear with the money. — [AllAfrica / Congo-Kinshasa, "Multiplicité de cas d'escroqueries à Kinshasa"](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202610070624.html)
- Local consumer advice for renting in Kinshasa: verify the property before paying, ask for proof of ownership, check the listing matches the property visited. — [Umvie, "5 astuces pour éviter de te faire arnaquer en louant à Kinshasa"](https://umvie.com/5-astuces-pour-eviter-de-te-faire-arnaquer-en-louant-a-kinshasa/)
- Kinshasa listings commonly carry entry costs like "3 mois de garantie, 3 mois anticipatif plus 1 mois de commission" — the upfront sums at stake. — [Mediacongo listing example](https://www.mediacongo.net/annonce-mediacongo-339410_immobilier_vente_location_toute_la_parcelle_a_louer_a_gombe_8000_dollars_contenant_une_maison_de_4_ch_piscine_grand_jardin_3_mois_de_garantie_3_mois_anticipatif_plus_1_mois_de_.html)
- Same pattern in Lagos: agents collect a registration fee, then "visit fees", and show a different house from the one advertised online. — [TechCabal (FR), "La promesse non tenue des services immobiliers numériques à Lagos"](https://techcabal.com/fr/2019/11/14/La-promesse-non-tenue-des-services-immobiliers-num%C3%A9riques-%C3%A0-Lagos/) (2019, older than preferred)
- Nigerian land-verification service LandSafe says Lagos, Abuja and Ogun account for over 500,000 property scams a year (self-reported by the company). — [TechCabal, 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/05/20/how-to-verify-land-or-property-in-nigeria-landsafe-is-the-answer/)
- Property Centre (Nigeria/Kenya) shows a "Verified" badge only after confirming the agent's or developer's identity and business details, while still advising due diligence before payment; verification is optional, so unverified listings coexist. — [Kenya Property Centre FAQ](https://kenyapropertycentre.com/frequently-asked-questions); [Property Centre help: verify an account](https://propertycentre.helpscoutdocs.com/article/43-how-to-verify-an-account)
- South Africa: WhatsApp-style verification spreading in property amid a PPRA (regulator) warning; BestAgent gives its Verified badge only to paid-tier agents (a conflict-of-interest risk). — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/whatsapp-style-verification-gains-ground-in-south-africas-property-sector-amid-ppra-warning-582290a)
- Other regional anti-scam proptech pitches: verified listings + live location maps + price history (ExpertListing, Lagos); 360° viewing to curb rental scams (Virety). — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/?p=344861); [ThisDay, Oct 2025](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2025/10/23/virety-unveils-360-property-viewing-technology-to-curb-rental-scams-in-africa)

### Inferences
- Strongest poster trust lines for Kinshasa address the named fears directly, e.g. "Agents vérifiés", "Les vraies photos du vrai logement", "Écrivez directement à l'agent sur WhatsApp", "Visitez avant de payer". Use only claims that are true of the product: in this codebase, "verified" means `agents.phone_verified_at` is set (a phone check), so a poster must not imply identity/ownership vetting unless that is actually done. "Sans frais de visite" should only be used if Lukka Place can guarantee agents never charge one; otherwise say what Lukka Place itself does not charge.
- A listing count ("X annonces") or review score is a trust signal only if it is the live, real number at print time (house rule against invented data).
- A visible verified tick next to an agent face/name is the regional convention customers will recognise (Property Centre model); a real agent photo with name is stronger proof than a stock face.
- Do not use AI-generated homes on any poster that also carries "vraies photos" claims — it would contradict the trust message.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on scam prevalence in Kinshasa, and no independent study measuring whether verified badges lift conversion or reduce fraud in African property marketplaces.
- Could not read the ACP/Umvie pages in full (host blocked); the details above come from search summaries.

## 3. How to use AI-generated imagery responsibly in real estate ads; what audiences think

### Takeaway
Regulators and industry bodies converge: AI imagery is acceptable for mood/brand scenes, but an image that could be read as the actual property, or as proof of what is offered, is misleading if it does not match reality, and a disclaimer does not cure that. Audiences are sceptical of AI in final ad visuals and especially of AI-generated people. For Lukka Place: real photos for anything presented as a home on the platform; AI only for clearly illustrative/editorial scenes, labelled, and never fake "customers" or "agents".

### Cited Findings
- UK ASA: AI images can be misleading if they don't reflect the product's actual performance/appearance; in the WiggyDog case, labelling footage as AI-generated or enhanced did not rescue an ad when the real product did not look or behave as depicted. — [Osborne Clarke on ASA/WiggyDog](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/asa-clamps-down-on-wiggydogs-exaggerated-robot-puppy-claims/); [ASA, "AI and deepfakes: four things advertisers need to know"](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ai-and-deepfakes-four-things-advertisers-need-to-know-before-they-hit-run.html)
- UK ASA (Henry's Boots): images of "Henry" were AI-generated and he was not a real person; the ads were found misleading (CAP Code 3.1 misleading advertising, 3.7 substantiation). — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/henrys-boots-gets-the-boot/); [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/person-s--unknown-a24-1267935-person-s--unknown.html)
- ASA position: no new rules for generative AI; existing misleading-advertising rules apply. — [Osborne Clarke, "Generative AI and the ASA: no new rules"](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/generative-ai-and-the-asa-no-new-rules/); [Lewis Silkin, June 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/06/18/asa-warns-on-ai-generated-content-and-deepfakes-102n4o1)
- Ireland (Cork property listing): an advertising regulator said AI-generated images on property listings are "not likely to mislead" if clearly flagged and real images are available close by (source names the body loosely; check whether UK ASA or Ireland's ASAI). — [TheJournal.ie](https://www.thejournal.ie/article.php?id=6960437)
- France: the ARPP published a practical sheet (2026) on labelling AI-generated/manipulated ad content, tied to EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations applying from 2 August 2026, with a three-criteria grid (degree of AI intervention, realism, impact on public perception) and example wording such as « Image générée par IA ». — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/102629); [ARPP practical guide (PDF)](https://www.arpp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/030826-PRACTICAL-GUIDE-LABELLING-AI.pdf)
- France's Conseil de l'éthique publicitaire (CEP) judged a blanket "made with AI" mention unjustified, but flagged deception risk where the image is a commitment (e.g. tourism — the place shown is what you get) and called for vigilance when AI makes a fictional depiction pass as real proof. — [CB News, CEP](https://www.cbnews.fr/conseil/cep-juge-injustifiee-mention-obligatoire-ia-les-publicites); [CB News, CEP vigilance](https://www.cbnews.fr/conseil/cep-appelle-vigilance-ethique-face-essor-ia-generative-publicite)
- Belgium's JEP (Francophone self-regulator) published a 2025 review on AI in advertising (French). — [JEP, RvR25 AI in Advertising (PDF)](https://www.jep.be/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/RvR25-AIinAdvertising-FR.pdf)
- US real estate: NAR's Code of Ethics requires a "true picture" in advertising; California rules (effective 1 January) require disclosure of digitally altered listing images plus access to the unaltered originals; New York's Department of State issued a 2025 alert on AI listing images; NorthstarMLS 2026 guidelines require labelling AI-enhanced/virtually staged images, pairing them with an unaltered image, and allow staging to add/remove furniture only, not change walls, floors, fixtures or views. No FTC-specific guidance on AI listing images was found. — [NAR, "When does enhancing listing photos go too far"](https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/when-does-enhancing-listing-photos-go-too-far); [NAR, legally risky](https://www.nar.realtor/magazine/real-estate-news/law-and-ethics/using-ai-to-enhance-listing-photos-can-be-legally-risky?random=3273867); [NorthstarMLS guidelines](https://northstarmls.com/insights/guidelines-for-virtual-staging-and-ai-enhanced-listing-photos/); [The Close](https://theclose.com/news-ai-listing-photo-disclosure/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/when-listings-lie-ai-staging-pushes-real-estate-into-an-ethics-gray-zone/)
- Audience attitudes — Bentley-Gallup 2026 (3,270 US adults, May 4–11, 2026): 79% recall seeing ads that looked AI-made; 75% accept AI for brainstorming/drafts if disclosed; 53% accept AI creating final ad text/images/video; ~62% (nearly two in three) find AI-generated people/voices unacceptable even when disclosed; 18–29-year-olds are the most sceptical. — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/713222/americans-aren-sold-businesses-using-ai-advertising.aspx); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/c/371464); [Fortune, Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/27/top-pollster-warns-customers-arent-sold-on-ai-advertising-not-free-lunch/)
- YouGov multi-market study: about 39% were fairly/very comfortable with AI-generated or AI-edited product images in ads; 60%+ uncomfortable with AI "virtual ambassadors" in France, Sweden and Britain. (Date not confirmed.) — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/49622-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-brand-advertising-how-do-consumers-around-the-world-feel-comfortable)
- Conflicting evidence on disclosure: a Korean experiment found disclosed AI images are penalised vs human-made ones (undisclosed, they reach parity); a Publicis Media/Yahoo survey (1,200 US consumers) reported that noticing an AI disclosure raised ad trustworthiness and brand trust. — [MDPI, Administrative Sciences 15(10):395](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3387/15/10/395); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/people-want-to-know-when-ai-is-used-in-ads-research-confirms/)
- About a quarter of respondents could correctly identify an AI image; ~83% say AI content labelling should be required by law (survey summarised by Quirks). — [Quirks](https://quirks.com/articles/consumer-trust-will-ai-erode-authenticity-in-marketing)

### Inferences
- Rule set for the 5 posters: (1) any image presented as "a home on Lukka Place" must be a real listing photo used with the agent's consent, unretouched beyond light correction; (2) AI may be used for editorial mood scenes (e.g. a family at a gate, a key handover) only if not presented as a specific listing, and labelled small but legibly « Image générée par IA » / « Illustration »; (3) never AI-generated "agents", "clients" or testimonials — Gallup shows the strongest rejection is for AI people, and ASA Henry's Boots shows a fake persona is misleading; (4) never an AI image next to a "vraies photos" claim.
- DRC has no AI-ad rule found, but French-language norms (ARPP/CEP/JEP, AI Act Art. 50) are the closest reference for a Francophone audience and are cheap to follow.
- A scam-wary audience is the worst place to be caught with a fake: one viral "this house doesn't exist" comment undermines the whole trust positioning.

### Gaps
- No survey found on African or Congolese attitudes to AI imagery in ads.
- No DRC (ARPCE/consumer-protection) guidance on advertising or AI imagery found.

## 4. Representation for a Congolese audience

### Takeaway
Evidence specific to Kinshasa is thin, but African advertising research consistently says authenticity and local cultural cues build trust and that mis-representation draws backlash. Use Congolese/Black people and recognisable Kinshasa built form (walled parcelles with metal gates, low houses with corrugated or tiled roofs, mid-rise blocks in Gombe) rather than Western suburban stock.

### Cited Findings
- Ipsos (South Africa): brands should respect local culture and traditions, which evolved on their own path rather than following Western trends. — [Ipsos, "What Black Panther teaches us about building emotional connections with the African consumer"](https://www.ipsos.com/en-za/node/425676)
- Strathmore Business School (Kenya, 2024): authenticity builds trust and loyalty among African consumers (Safaricom, MTN, Fan Milk, Nando's cited; opinion piece, not a study). — [Strathmore](https://sbs.strathmore.edu/?p=36745)
- South African studies: race in advertising remains sensitive; brands faced social-media backlash for ads seen as racially insensitive; TV ads over-represented the White minority. — [Communicare / UJ journal](https://journals.uj.ac.za/index.php/jcsa/article/download/2001/1285/5124); [SciELO, Communicatio v30](https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/comm/v30/05.pdf)
- Kinshasa's centre (Gombe) has taller apartment blocks, banks and shops; poorer areas are mostly houses with corrugated-iron roofs and apartment buildings on unpaved streets. — [Universalis, Kinshasa: organisation spatiale](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/kinshasa/2-organisation-spatiale-de-la-ville/)
- Kinshasa listings routinely advertise « parcelle clôturée » with « portail » as selling points (fenced plot, gate). — [Expat.com listing, N'sele](https://www.expat.com/fr/immobilier/afrique/republique-democratique-du-congo/kinshasa/42-maisons-a-vendre/815106-parcelle-cloturee-avec-grand-maison-a-vendre-a-terre-jaune-nsele-pres-du-boulevard-lumumba.html); [Mediacongo listing, Bumbu](https://www.mediacongo.net/annonce-mediacongo-349624_immobilier_vente_location_gs13_immo_sarl_met_a_votre_disposition_la_vente_de_cette_parcelle_sur_l_av_kokolo_direction_birmanie_c_bumbu_bel_endroit_dimension_7_20m_prix_90_000_a.html)
- Academic work on Kinshasa housing typology (the parcelle and its house): Martine Kapita Kamba thesis (ULiège). — [ORBi ULiège](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/236819/1/KapitaKamba_Martine_These.pdf)

### Inferences
- People: Congolese adults in the roles customers recognise — a young couple or family at their own gate, a woman professional in Gombe, an agent holding a phone with WhatsApp. Natural, everyday Kinshasa dress, not Western corporate stock. Real people (staff, real agents with consent) beat AI people (see §3).
- Places: walled parcelle with painted metal gate and a tree; low house with veranda; a mid-rise block in Gombe; avoid lawns-and-picket-fence suburbia, snow-white Scandinavian interiors, or Lagos/Abidjan skylines passed off as Kinshasa.
- The repo's own rules apply to posters: "Parcelle" vs "Appartement" must be used correctly; places written "quartier, commune"; French "référence" never "repère".

### Gaps
- No Kantar/Meta Africa study on stock imagery or local representation in Central/Francophone Africa found; no DRC-specific creative testing found.
- No authoritative visual description of Gombe/Limete residential typologies found online (only theses and listings).

## 5. Typography and colour: legibility on phones, contrast, French copy length

### Takeaway
White on #1650C5 has a WCAG contrast ratio of about 7.0:1 (passes AA and AAA for normal text), so the brand blue works as both background and headline colour on white. French runs about 15–30% longer than English, so headlines must be written short in French from the start, not translated.

### Cited Findings
- Computed WCAG 2.x contrast (local calculation, standard relative-luminance formula): white #FFFFFF on #1650C5 = 7.03:1; #1650C5 on off-white #F5F5F0 = 6.43:1; near-black #111111 on white = 18.88:1. WCAG thresholds: 4.5:1 normal text / 3:1 large text (AA), 7:1 normal (AAA). — calculation in this session; thresholds per [W3C WCAG 2.1, 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/#contrast-minimum)
- Third-party Meta guidance: high contrast, short text, text kept off the main subject for mobile readability. — [Straight North](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-best-practices-that-convert/)
- French text typically expands 15–30% vs English (15–20% for US English→French per one translation firm); short strings expand the most in relative terms; designers plan 30–40% headroom. — [W3C, Text size in translation](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size/index.en); [e2f, taux de foisonnement](https://e2f.com/news/taux-de-foisonnement-expansion-rate); [DEV, French localization broke our layout](https://dev.to/jam-techcirkle/french-localization-broke-our-layout-and-it-was-entirely-our-fault-51p0)

### Inferences
- Headline ≤ 6–7 French words, set very large (on a 1080 px-wide canvas, roughly 90–140 px cap height range for 2–3 lines), tight leading, sans-serif bold/black; body ≤ 2 short lines; one CTA (e.g. « Trouvez sur lukkaplace.com » or « Écrivez-nous sur WhatsApp »).
- Colour system: white field + near-black headline + one royal-blue block (logo band or CTA pill) keeps the editorial look while staying on-brand; or invert for one poster (blue field, white headline) — both pass AAA.
- Avoid thin weights and small grey text: much of the audience views on budget Android screens in bright outdoor light (inference; no DRC device data gathered).
- Write accents correctly (é, è, à, ç) and check the chosen typeface supports them.

### Gaps
- No DRC-specific data on screen sizes, data costs or reading habits for ad text was gathered.
- No published test of French headline length vs performance found.
